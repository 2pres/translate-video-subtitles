# Subtitle quality bar

## Source correction

- Treat ASR as a draft. Re-listen around low-confidence text, names, numbers, commands, acronyms, quotations, and sentence boundaries.
- Preserve meaning carried by hesitation or emphasis, but remove filler that harms subtitle readability when it adds no meaning.
- Keep code, paths, flags, shortcuts, model names, and brand capitalization exact.
- Never repair unclear speech by inventing a plausible sentence. Mark a cue for review or report the uncertainty.

## Translation

- Translate the speaker's intended meaning and register, not English word order.
- Prefer natural, compact target-language phrasing that can be read once at playback speed.
- Keep terminology consistent throughout the video. Build a tiny glossary before translating long or technical videos.
- Preserve jokes, contrast, uncertainty, and negation. Check pronoun references across cue boundaries.
- Do not translate proper nouns or developer terms unless an established localized form clearly helps the audience.

## Timing and segmentation

- Align cue starts and ends with audible speech; allow a small reading tail without carrying text far into silence.
- Aim for 1.0–6.0 seconds per cue. Treat cues under 0.7 seconds or over 8 seconds as review items.
- Merge consecutive fragments when they form one short thought. Split when a card contains two ideas or becomes visually dense.
- Prefer clause and phrase boundaries. Do not split a name, number and unit, phrasal verb, or tightly bound modifier.
- Avoid adjacent cards where the first ends with a dangling conjunction and the next contains only the completion.

## Reading density

- Chinese target: normally no more than two short lines and roughly 18 display units per second.
- English original: normally no more than two short lines and roughly 24 characters per second.
- A bilingual card may use two lines per language only when necessary; resegment before reducing font size.
- Use punctuation that improves instant comprehension. Avoid decorative punctuation and long on-screen paragraphs.

## Visual treatment

- Put the translated line above the corrected original line.
- Use a calm sans-serif with CJK coverage. Keep the translation slightly larger and the original quieter.
- Use white or warm-white text, a restrained dark outline/shadow, and no large opaque caption panel by default.
- Size from the shorter video dimension so portrait captions do not become oversized.
- Keep a 6–9% bottom safe margin. Raise captions when persistent lower thirds, platform UI, or speaker labels occupy that area.
- Match an established product style when one exists; readability outranks decorative styling.

## QA pass

Review the finished video, not only the subtitle source:

1. opening title and first spoken sentence;
2. fastest or densest passage;
3. names, technical terms, numbers, and commands;
4. scene transitions and lower thirds;
5. final sentence and end frame.

Listen while checking timing. A still contact sheet catches layout defects but cannot prove sync or translation quality.
